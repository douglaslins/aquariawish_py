# Pulls Tracker, start screen

Open `index.html` in a desktop browser (Chrome, Edge or Firefox). No server or build step. Scripts: `js/data.js` (fixed data), `js/banners.js` (banner snapshot), `js/banners-base.js` (points its paths at the data folder), `js/api.js` (data layer), `js/app.js` (Main screen), `js/views.js` (Dashboard, Banners, History, Stats, dialog), `js/panels.js` (Settings modal tabs), `js/window.js` (minimize and close buttons, tells Python the first screen is ready), `loading.html` with `css/loading.css` and `js/loading.js` (loading panel).

## What is here
- Typography (v22): everything outside `.banner` (top bar and tabs, rail, goal and result area, modals, toasts) uses the Zenless Zone Zero settings (Mona Sans, weights 500 and 800, width 106%) for every game. The banner keeps the font of the selected game: the game values are set again on `.banner` in `css/app.css` (section "Typography"), so name plates and the combined display are unchanged. The tabs switch to the short names at 640px or less (the full names in the wider Zenless font need about 605px).
- Shell: top bar with the three game tabs, sidebar with five tiles in one column (Dashboard, Banners, History, Stats, Settings; 70 x 70 px, glyph above the name, 8 pt labels, 4 px corners, no border), account and sync tiles below.
- Main screen (S0): banner stage with previous and next arrows, a name plate floating over the art (element icon, name, stars, description), goal selector, owned currency and tickets, Needed or Guaranteed!, pulls needed, pull ruler.
- Goal dropdowns and the character + weapon display (v19): both dropdowns are always usable and both have a "None" option. A character banner opens with the weapon dropdown on None, a weapon banner with the character dropdown on None (the tier and rank you chose are remembered). One dropdown on None keeps the other off None, so both can never be None. Setting the dropdown that is on None to a value shows the character and the weapon together (`data-mode="combo"` on `#banner`; markup in `#combo`, styles at the end of `css/app.css`). The banner arrows and the pager disappear; each name plate has its own pair of arrows: the character arrows cycle the character banners of the game, the weapon arrows the weapon banners (the pair shown first is the character or weapon you were on and the last one you viewed of the other kind). Setting the weapon dropdown to None goes back to the single display of the character, setting the character dropdown to None to the single display of the weapon. The layout is drawn in code from the four Star Rail "Event Warp" references: character art centered at 32.5% across with its plate under it, a tilted weapon card in a glass case at 71.5%, its plate under it, the title at the top center and a bar in the element's color at the bottom. The side assets of the references (round icons of the rate-up characters, tilted cards of the rate-up weapons, the character banner switcher) are not drawn: only the chosen character and the chosen weapon are shown. Genshin and Zenless Zone Zero have no such banner in the game; they use the same layout with their own art, element icons and rank marks, and a title of their own ("Event Wish", "Signal Search"). The Zenless version uses the Zenless base background with the element effect of the chosen character (`--zzz-fx-opacity`) and the Zenless name plate (black plate, white name field, element icon in the strip, starburst S) for both the character and the W-Engine. The time chip shows the earlier end of the two banners. Only the Star Rail version has the glass case and the tilted card around the weapon art; the Genshin and Zenless versions show the weapon art alone (upright, centered over its plate), and the Genshin weapon art is `--combo-genshin-weapon-scale` (2) times its former size. Hand-editable in the `<style id="manual-tweaks">` block of `index.html`: `--combo-art-scale`, `--combo-art-x`, `--combo-card-scale`, `--combo-genshin-weapon-scale`.
- Calculation for the combined goal: the worst case of the character goal (copies x 2 x 90) and of the weapon goal (copies x 2 x 80) are added, each reduced by its own pity and guarantee (the character banner's counter and the weapon banner's counter, read per slide `group` from the history), and the owned pulls are subtracted once. The ruler has a gap after every block (blocks of 90 for the character part, 80 for the weapon part). `window.PT_calcGoal(parts, currency, tickets)` is the pure function. The history source (Python backend, not connected yet) must give both counters for the game, in the shape of `sampleHistory.pity` in `js/data.js`; the banner script `banner_tracker_test.py` needs no change for this.
- Name plate (`.plate`, `.identity`): sized from one variable, `--F` in `css/app.css`. Colors are tokens on `:root` (`--plate-dark`, `--plate-title`, `--plate-title-ink`). Character art enlargement: `--art-scale` (1.5).
- Settings modal and the other screens: see "Screens (v23)" below.
- Star Rail character banners (`data-layout="hsr-char"`) have their own layout, built from the reference mock-ups. A base image drawn in code (light panel with lattice strip, black edge, "Character Event Warp" tab, accent bar, top-right bracket; `.hsr-base` in `css/app.css`) sits over a gradient layer chosen by the character's element. The reference gradient is the Physical element, measured from the mock-up: a fixed navy top shade and blue bottom tint, plus a left-to-right ramp in three stops. The character art is 50% larger than in v14: `--hsr-art-scale` (1.5) in the `<style id="manual-tweaks">` block of `index.html` sets its size as a multiple of the old size (1 = old size), scaled around the art's center.

- Zenless Zone Zero banners (`data-layout="zzz"`, characters and W-Engines) use the supplied base image `assets/zzz/Base_Background.webp`. - In a browser (no Python backend), the screens run on the sample provider of `js/api.js`: without `?state=synced` the account has no pulls (empty states and the first-run steps show), with it a made-up history is served and every screen says "Sample data, not your pulls". ## Banner data in the application (v27)
`index.html` loads the banner data from `PT_DATA_BASE + "js/banners.js"`. `PT_DATA_BASE` is empty in a browser (the file shipped next to the page, a snapshot) and is the application's data folder when the page is opened with `#data=<folder URL>` (or `?data=` in a browser) (the load tasks wrote `js/banners.js` and `assets/art/` there). `js/banners-base.js` then puts the folder in front of the relative `art` and `icon` paths of every slide and rate-up item; remote addresses and `null` stay as they are. `banner_tracker_test.py` still works on its own: run without options it writes into this folder (`js/banners.js`, `assets/art`), which refreshes the snapshot.

## Loading panel (v26)
`loading.html` is the page of the loading panel, a frameless window of 803 x 230 px: a 230 x 230 area on the left (donut 135 px with the percentage inside, task label under it, 0.6 rem, up to two lines) and a 573 x 230 area on the right for the banner. Files: `css/loading.css` (sizes `--side`, `--banner-w`, `--h`, ring color `--accent`), `js/loading.js`.
- Python drives it with `PT_LOAD.set({ done, total, fraction, label })`: the donut shows (done + fraction) / total, the label is the running task. `PT_LOAD.set` is called through `evaluate_js`.
- Banner: `assets/loading/banner.png` (573 x 230; `.webp`, `.jpg`, `.jpeg` are tried after it). See `assets/loading/README.txt`.
- Preview in a browser: `loading.html?demo=1` (`&speed=0.3` is faster) steps through sample labels. The real labels are in `pt/loader.py`.
- The whole panel is a drag region (`pywebview-drag-region`), so it can be moved.
- When the main page is ready, `js/window.js` calls `PT_API.uiReady()`; Python then shows the main window. (A hidden window does not run `requestAnimationFrame`, so it waits with timers, up to 2 s for the banner art.)

## Window controls (v24)
For the frameless pywebview window (`frameless=True`) the page draws its own minimize and close buttons in the top right corner of the top bar (46 px wide, the height of the bar; close turns red on hover). Code: `js/window.js`, styles in `css/app.css` ("Window controls").
- Shown when the page runs in the Python application (`#backend=1`; `?backend=1` in a browser). In a browser add `?frameless=1` to preview them; `?frameless=0` hides them for a window that has its own frame. The state is `body[data-frameless]`.
- Minimize calls `PT_API.windowMinimize()`, close calls `PT_API.windowClose()`. In a browser they show "Window buttons work in the desktop application."
- Close asks first (dialog "Close Pulls Tracker?", buttons Keep open / Close) when a sync is running (Close then calls `syncCancel()` before `windowClose()`) or when the Settings tab has unsaved changes. Otherwise it closes at once.
- A modal dialog blocks everything outside it, so each dialog (`#settingsModal`, `#dlg`) carries its own copy of the buttons, fixed to the window corner. In the frameless window dialogs also stay at least one bar height (56 px) from the top and bottom edge, so they never cover the buttons.
- Resizing the window: eight handles (`.rz-h`, built by `js/window.js`, also inside each dialog) call `windowStartResize(edge)` on pointer down. See the main README for how the backend follows the mouse and the page-driven fallback.
- Moving the window: the whole top bar moves it, except its buttons. `div.drag.pywebview-drag-region` is a layer that fills the bar (`position: absolute; inset: 0`) and lies behind the brand button, the tabs and the window buttons (`.topbar > .brand, .tabs, .winctl` are `position: relative; z-index: 1`; the `.tabs` strip has `pointer-events: none` so its gaps reach the layer, its `.tab` buttons have `pointer-events: auto`). The layer is not the parent of the buttons because pywebview starts a drag for every element inside a drag region. Do not put `pywebview-drag-region` on `.topbar` itself. Create the window with `easy_drag=False` so that only this region moves it (with easy drag, every part of the page drags and text cannot be selected).
- Backend side (`pt/window.py` in the project folder): `windowMinimize` is `window.minimize()`, `windowClose` is `window.destroy()` on the pywebview window; the window is created with `easy_drag=False`.

## Inside the application (v25)
With `backend=1` a method the Python side does not have yet is answered like this: the read methods (`state`, `getSettings`, `dashboard`, `windowStats`, `windowPulls`, `pastBanners`, `history`, `stats`, `storage`, `firstRun`, `sources`) return what the sample provider returns for an account with no pulls, so every screen shows its empty state; every other method returns `{ unavailable: true }` and the screen says "not available yet in this version of the application" (instead of "works in the desktop application", which is the text in a browser). As each method is added on the Python side it takes over.

## Data layer and backend contract
`js/api.js` defines `window.PT_API`; every method returns a Promise. With `backend=1` (the application opens the page as `index.html#backend=1`) the page waits for pywebview (`pywebviewready`) and each method calls `window.pywebview.api.<same name>`; a method the backend does not have answers `{ unavailable: true }` and the screen shows a short message. `window.PT_RULES` holds the game rules the screens need (pity caps, banner type names, rarity labels, pull cost); `window.PT_EVENTS` carries events that arrive during a call: the backend calls `window.PT_EVENTS.emit({...})` through `evaluate_js`.

**A method that raised.** The Python side never raises to the page (`safe` in `pt/api.py`): when a method fails it answers its fallback plus `error` (the text) and `failed: true`. `js/api.js` turns an answer with `failed: true` into a rejected Promise and announces it (`pt:failed`, which `js/app.js` shows as a message), so a screen shows the error instead of drawing the fallback as if it were the answer (an empty history, "works in the desktop application") and a click handler that does not catch it still says something. An answer that only has an `error` text (a refused import, a folder outside the data folder) is not marked: the screen shows that text itself. A map keyed by id (`windowStats`), a list (`pastBanners`) and a boolean (`setActiveAccount`, `removeAccount`, `saveNickname`, `syncCancel`) have no room for the marks and answer their plain fallback. A page opened from a file does not get `unhandledrejection` for a rejection made in another script file, which is why the announcement is an event of its own.

**Files and links dropped on the window.** WebView2 opens a dropped file or link in the window, and pywebview gives that page the application's methods; `js/app.js` cancels `dragover` and `drop`, so dropping does nothing. `index.html` and `js/loading.js` accept `#data=` only when it is an address made of URL characters (the application sends `file:///...` with every special character coded); anything else is ignored.

## Banner data
`js/banners.js` holds the current banners. It is written by `banner_tracker_test.py`, which sits next to this folder:

    python banner_tracker_test.py

The script runs three steps for each game: 1. find the current banners (calendar), 2. list the characters and weapons in them, 3. fetch the art and the full description of every character. It saves the images under `assets/art/<game>/<id>-art.*` and `<id>-icon.*`, writes `js/banners.js`, and writes `banner_tracker_test.html`, a report of the three steps. Options: `--ui-dir` (default: `pulls_tracker_ui` next to the script), `--no-export` (report only), `--embed-all-art`, `--out`, `--lang`, `--base-url`, `--zzz-art`, `--zzz-art-probe`.

Zenless Zone Zero art: the script tries Prydwen's full-body cutout first (`https://cdn.prydwen.gg/images/zenless-zone-zero/characters/<name>_full.webp`; a fan site's CDN, not an official source, and the file name is guessed from the agent's name: `velina`, `zhu-yuan`, `zhu_yuan`, `zhuyuan`, `zhu`; a name that does not match goes into `PRYDWEN_STEMS` at the top of the script). When that fails it falls back to HoYoLAB's `role_vertical_painting` (the calendar icon URL with its folder swapped), which is a banner-shaped crop (684 x 356 for Phoenix) and shows only the bust. The other files the script knows are nanoka's `select` (IconRoleSelect, a 256 x 250 icon) and `mindscape1` to `mindscape3` (Mindscape_<id>_1 to 3, 1512 x 1080 cinema art with its own background). `--zzz-art LIST` sets the order (default `prydwen,painting,select,mindscape1`). `--zzz-art-probe` downloads all variants for the featured agents, saves them next to the used art as `<id>-art-<variant>`, prints their pixel sizes in the console and shows them side by side in the report. The start screen places a wide image (wider than 1.4 : 1) over the whole stage and an upright one at the right (`art-tall`), centered across the banner and 168% of its height (`--zzz-art-left` 50%, `--zzz-art-height` 168%, `--zzz-art-top` -6%, so a larger art grows downward and keeps the head in view). The name plate position is `--zzz-plate-left` and `--zzz-plate-top`. All five are in the `<style id="manual-tweaks">` block of `index.html`; a wide image (bust crop, cinema art) is not affected by the art ones.

The shipped `js/banners.js` is a snapshot of the run of 2026-10-25 14:17 UTC (Genshin 7.1, Star Rail 4.6, ZZZ 3.3). It stops showing a banner after its end time. Run the script again to refresh it.

Each image loads from the saved file first, then from the URL stored next to it (`artRemote`, `iconRemote`), and character art falls back to the square icon if neither loads. Only the files named in the snapshot are in `assets/art`; the rest load from the network until the script is run.

A slide is one featured 5-star item of a current banner. Fields: `id, group, kind, banner, bannerName, version, start, end, itemId, name, rank, icon, iconRemote, rateUp[]`, and for characters `element, role, art, artRemote, title, titleSource, desc, descSource`; every slide also has `bannerTitle` (the game's own banner name, or null) and `bannerTitleSource` (`calendar`, `constant` or null). The text on the name plate is `title` when there is one (Genshin only: "Void Star", read from genshin-db, else from the nanoka detail file) and `desc` otherwise. Featured weapons also get `art` and `artRemote`: the full-size art (Genshin `UI_Gacha_EquipIcon_*`, tried on nanoka, Enka and yatta in that order; Star Rail `lightconemaxfigures/<id>`). The calendar icon is only a small square crop. Zenless Zone Zero W-Engines have no full-size source yet and keep the icon. `rateUp` lists the 4-star items of the banner (not shown on screen yet).

`js/data.js` keeps the fixed data only: the `?preview=newest` slides and the `?state=synced` sample pity.

## Address parameters
Address parameters can be in the query string (`?state=synced`) or in the fragment (`#state=synced`); the application uses the fragment, because a `?` in a file address does not work in the Windows WebView2 host. `js/params.js` merges both into `window.PT_PARAMS`.

`#perf=1` (the backend's `--perf`: `js/perf.js` writes responsiveness measurements to the log)  `?game=genshin|hsr|zzz`  `?state=synced`  `?preview=newest` (Vesna and Vodyanitsa, to see Anemo and Hydro with real art)  `?now=2026-10-24T12:00:00Z`  `?modal=settings|sync|transfer|data` (opens the Settings modal on that tab; add `&auto=1` on `sync` to start a sync)  `?view=dashboard|banners|history|stats` (opens that screen)  `?backend=1` (use the Python backend through pywebview instead of the sample provider)  `?frameless=1|0` (show or hide the window buttons)  `?demo=sync`, `?fail=expired|cache|rate|uid`, `?speed=0.1` (sample sync run: succeed without stored pulls, end with that error, run faster)
